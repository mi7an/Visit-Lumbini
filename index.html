Visit Lumbini

Welcome to the Land of Peace

Located in the serene Terai plains of Nepal, Lumbini is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a place of immense historical and spiritual significance. It is here, in a tranquil garden setting, that Siddhartha Gautama was born in 623 B.C., who would later become the Lord Buddha, the founder of Buddhism. A pilgrimage to this hallowed ground is more than just a trip, it's an immersive experience that connects you to the very origins of a global religion, offering a rare opportunity for self-reflection and inner peace.

World Peace Pagoda

Discover Lumbini's Must-See Sights

The Ashoka Pillar, erected in 249 B.C. by Emperor Ashoka, stands as a crucial and tangible piece of evidence confirming Lumbini as the Buddha's birthplace. Its ancient inscription serves as a testament to the site's authenticity and historical importance. A short walk from the pillar, the brilliant white World Peace Pagoda stands as a magnificent symbol of global peace and harmony. Built by Japanese Buddhists, this dome-shaped structure provides a peaceful place for quiet reflection, surrounded by landscaped gardens and a sense of profound calm.

A Global Hub of Faith

A Global Hub of Faith

The Monastic Zone in Lumbini is a testament to the worldwide reverence for the Buddha. It features a stunning collection of monasteries, temples, and stupas, each built by different Buddhist communities from across the globe, including China, Thailand, Sri Lanka, and Germany. Each structure reflects the unique architectural style and spiritual traditions of its home country, creating a diverse and colorful spiritual landscape that is truly a sight to behold. This area is perfect for serene walks, where you can admire the intricate designs and feel the unity of faith from around the world.

Plan Your Journey

Plan Your Journey to Peace

Getting to Lumbini is a seamless experience, whether you're traveling from within Nepal or internationally. The nearest major hub is Gautam Buddha International Airport (BWA), located in Bhairahawa, just a short 30-minute drive from the sacred site. For those preferring to travel by land, comfortable bus services are available from major cities in Nepal and neighboring India. The best time to visit is from October to March, when the weather is cool and pleasant, making it ideal for exploring the vast grounds and attractions.
